What can count as
off-the-job learning?

Use this OTJ / DDT guide to plan purposeful learning, recognise the difference between training and routine work, and write a useful learning record. Examples cover creating, editing and managing content.

Start with the learning

Four checks before you log it.

Off-the-job training develops the knowledge, skills and behaviours required by your apprenticeship. It can take place at work: the key is planned learning away from normal productive duties. Agree the learning with your programme lead and employer and include it in your training plan.

01

New learning for you

Identify a real gap. A new technique in a familiar tool can qualify; repeating a skill you already have does not become training just because it produces a new piece of work.

02

Relevant to the standard

Link the learning to the Content Creator KSBs. Being useful to your job is not enough by itself: the training must be required by the apprenticeship.

03

Within paid working time

Plan learning during your normal working hours. Personal study outside those hours does not count automatically. Exceptional arrangements need agreement and compensation.

04

An eligible training activity

Teaching, guided practical training, relevant mentoring, assignments and planned revision can qualify. Initial assessment, progress reviews, tests and routine work are excluded.

Your role changes the context, not the standard.

A creator, editor or manager can learn through the same subject. Your record should show what you learned and did. Commissioning someone else’s work does not replace the practical skills you need to develop.

The grey areas

The purpose and the new learning make the difference.

A task label does not decide whether time qualifies. Identify the training within the activity and record that portion accurately.

I spent three hours on a live project. Can I log all of it?

Record only the eligible planned learning, not the whole project. For example, 25 minutes of coaching and 35 minutes of a guided exercise may be training; the remaining two hours of ordinary delivery are not. Do not count the same time twice.

I already use the tool. Can I still learn something new?

Yes, where there is a relevant gap in the apprenticeship requirements. Learning an unfamiliar editing or accessibility technique can qualify. Repeating a familiar edit, or practising a skill already acquired, does not automatically qualify.

Does reading, watching a tutorial or listening to a podcast count?

It can, when it is relevant, planned learning during normal working hours. Identify what the resource teaches you and record the actual learning time. Scrolling, general browsing, commuting and entertainment are not automatically training.

What about research or reviewing a campaign?

Learning a new research, segmentation or evaluation method can qualify. Routine competitor checks, regular analytics reviews and already-familiar reporting methods are productive work. A report or meeting becomes training only where there is a distinct eligible learning activity.

Can shadowing, coaching or mentoring count?

Receiving planned coaching or shadowing for a specific new skill can qualify. General induction, pastoral support and a routine catch-up do not. If you are the mentor, teaching a skill you already have is not automatically new learning for you.

What about assignments, revision and portfolio preparation?

Writing a planned assignment can qualify where you continue to develop relevant new skills through it. Planned revision can qualify when it meets the four checks. Collecting old files, compiling a portfolio or completing an assessment is not new training.

Do meetings and progress reviews count?

Ordinary business meetings and apprenticeship progress reviews do not qualify as training. If a separate teaching session takes place around a meeting or review, record the eligible training portion separately, not the whole meeting.

Can I log learning during lunch or in the evening?

Do not count voluntary personal study or an unpaid lunch break as normal working-hours training. If training exceptionally has to take place outside normal hours, agree it in advance with the programme and employer, with compensation such as paid time or time off in lieu.

Does a certificate, new tool or one KSB tag prove it counts?

No. A certificate can support your record, but eligibility depends on the actual learning, relevance, timing and activity. KSB tags help you plan. A first use of a tool, or a new output, does not by itself prove new learning.

How many hours should I complete?

Use the agreed total and delivery plan for your own apprenticeship. Requirements depend on your start date and applicable rules. Ask your programme lead to confirm your target; do not assume a universal weekly figure from an older guide.

Activities that may qualify

  • Planned teaching, a workshop or an interactive online session.
  • Guided practical training that addresses a relevant skill gap.
  • Specific coaching, mentoring, shadowing or an industry visit.
  • Planned self-directed learning and assignments relevant to the standard.
  • Eligible learning support or revision that meets the four checks.

Activities to leave out

  • Routine production, publishing, admin and repeated familiar tasks.
  • Initial assessment and apprenticeship onboarding.
  • Progress reviews, examinations and on-programme testing.
  • Standalone English and maths qualification time.
  • Training outside the standard or voluntary study in your own time.
  • Time spent gathering existing portfolio files or simply entering the log.

Make the learning visible

A useful log explains what changed in your understanding.

Record actual learning time and identify how it fits your agreed plan. A short, specific entry is more useful than a long account of everything you did at work.

When and how long?
Date, start and finish time, eligible learning duration and confirmation that it took place in agreed working time. Exclude routine work and log-entry admin.
What was the new learning?
The gap or technique you were developing, rather than just the title of a task.
Which KSBs did it support?
Choose the relevant codes and explain the connection. Use your own programme requirements.
What did you do and learn?
The training activity, what you now understand, and any useful feedback.
How will you use it?
The next application or improvement in your work. You do not have to claim a business result that has not happened yet.
What supports the record?
The resource, session or tutor, notes, exercise, feedback or other relevant evidence. Keep confidential material in approved systems.

Weak: “Spent an hour on Canva.”
Stronger: “Learned a new method for organising a brand template through a tutorial and guided exercise. I can now explain which elements should be fixed and which should be editable.”

Learning evidence and assessment evidence serve different purposes.

An OTJ record shows eligible training took place. Assessment evidence demonstrates your own competence against the required criteria. A resource link or learning log alone does not automatically meet an assessment descriptor.
